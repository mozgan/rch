# standard_hilbert_vectors.jsonl Provenance

Status: definition-pinned in-repository oracle, not an external third-party oracle.
This mirrors the status of `compact_hilbert_vectors.provenance.md`.

## Why a third-party index comparison is not possible here

It is tempting to validate these vectors by diffing them against another Hilbert
library. That comparison is **not meaningful for 3D**, and a mismatch there is not
evidence of a defect:

> "The properties that make Hilbert's curve unique in two dimensions are shared by
> **10 694 807 structurally different space-filling curves in three dimensions**"
> — Herman Haverkort, *An inventory of three-dimensional Hilbert space-filling
> curves*, arXiv:1109.2323. The count is modulo rotation, reflection **and reversal**.

In 2D the Hilbert curve is essentially unique up to symmetry, so cross-library
comparison works. In 3D it is not: any two implementations may legitimately realise
different members of that family.

`Hilbert3Standard` follows the entry-point/direction formulation of Hamilton
(TR CS-2006-07) and Hamilton & Rau-Chaplin (2008), which is what makes the compact
variant in `hilbert3_compact.hpp` a consistent restriction of it. Other libraries
commonly implement Skilling's (2004) algorithm, which yields a different member.

Measured, 2026-08-11: against the Python `hilbertcurve` 2.0.5 package (Skilling), the
two orders agree at `bits = 1` under an axis relabelling, and at `bits = 2, 3` no
element of the 48 octahedral symmetries — with or without index reversal — maps one
onto the other. Both were separately confirmed to be valid Hilbert curves (below).
That is the expected outcome for two distinct members of Haverkort's family.

## What these vectors are validated against instead

The three defining properties of a true Hilbert curve, checked **exhaustively** rather
than sampled, for `bits = 0..5` (up to 32 768 cells):

1. **Bijection** — `decode` visits every cell of the `2^bits` cube exactly once, and
   `encode(decode(i)) == i`.
2. **Adjacency** — consecutive indices map to face-adjacent cells, i.e. the L1 distance
   between successive cells is exactly 1.
3. **Recursive containment** — at every level `L`, each `2^L`-subcube is visited in one
   contiguous run of exactly `(N/2^L)^3` consecutive indices. This is the self-similarity
   that distinguishes a Hilbert curve from an arbitrary adjacency-preserving Hamiltonian
   path on the grid, and it was verified at all levels for `bits = 2, 3, 4`.

The in-repo tests covering these are `HilbertIntrinsics.StandardIsABijectionAndRoundTrips`,
`HilbertIntrinsics.StandardVisitsGridNeighboursInSequence`,
`StandardHilbertOracle.ConsecutiveDecodedCellsAreFaceAdjacent`, and
`StandardHilbert3.DecodeEncodeRoundTripExhaustiveSmallDomains`.

## Machine-verification command

```text
cmake --build build/debug --target test_standard_hilbert_oracle -j 4
./build/debug/tests/oracle/test_standard_hilbert_oracle
```

## Limitation

This is a definition-pinned regression/provenance check. It is not claimed to be
independently generated by a third-party implementation, and per the Haverkort result
above it could not be without also fixing a choice of curve variant.
